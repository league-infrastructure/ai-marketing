# The League of Amazing Programmers
## Campaign Slogans — Working Inventory

*Vetted and accepted slogans, grouped by campaign purpose. A living document — revise as the work continues. Each slogan carries a note on the moment or audience it serves.*

---

## Host a Meeting

- **Bring the Future Home**
  - First-time host. Emotional, aspirational headline. "Bring… home" is a verb aimed at the parent; "the future" carries the career promise without saying anything dry.
- **Your kids, our robots**
  - The deal stated plainly — almost a handshake. Steer the surrounding copy toward hosting, not drop-off, so it doesn't read as "we entertain your kid."
- **That was fun! Let's do it again**
  - Repeat host. Handed warm at the end of a successful event. The "That was fun!" half supplies the emotion before the ask. Lightest card in the set — the host is already sold, so the back is just dates and a QR.
- **No genius required. Just eight kids and an afternoon.**
  - Permission angle. For the parent who thinks their kid isn't a "STEM kid." The line that separates the League from every other STEM nonprofit — and the riskiest in tone.
- **Engineering Everywhere**
  - Geographic-expansion host pitch: bring a Tech Club to your part of town. "Everywhere" is a thing the host makes happen, not a claim the org makes about itself.

## Donate

### Theme: Free is not Free

- **Free for them. Funded by you.**
  - Lead donation line. Two beats, parallel structure, names both parties.
- **The lessons are free. The instructor has rent.**
  - The honest, concrete one. "The instructor has rent" is the sharpest image in the set — it makes the cost real.

### Theme: Give the Gift of STEM

- **Don't buy them a thing; buy them a future.**
  - Gift occasion. The cleanest statement of the gift thesis.
- **Less stuff. More skills.**
  - Gift occasion, short. Anti-consumerist; lands with parents drowning in plastic toys.
- **A great career starts with "Hello World."**
  - Technical-audience warmth. "Hello World" is an insider handshake for the technical parent.

### Theme: The next Engineer could come from anywhere

- **The next Engineer could come from anywhere.**
  - Title line, for the parent who just watched their own kid at a free event. The equity argument as optimism, not grievance: talent is randomly distributed, opportunity is not — and "anywhere" quietly includes the donor's own kid, so it isn't about "those kids over there."
- **Every student in San Diego should have a shot at a tech career.**
  - Sub line, pairs under the title above.

### Theme: STEM Requires Programming

- **Every STEM Career Runs on Code.**
  - The rational argument. Confident, true, concrete — for the donor who needs the case made.
- **The Language of Science is Software.**
  - The authorship / AI-proof argument. "Software" (over "Python") puts the kid in the author's chair, not the student's seat — they build the thing and direct the tools that build it. Future-proofs the case against "isn't AI doing this now?"

## Advocate

*The Advocate is the distinct ask — they don't host, teach, or give. They connect and they vouch. The value is their credibility lent to us: a warm introduction is worth more than any cold outreach. The pitch is not "do work," it's "open a door only you can open."*

- **Take us to your leader**
  - Lead Advocate line, delivered by one of our robots styled as a friendly flying saucer. "Your leader" names the target without listing targets — every advocate pictures their own principal, pastor, HR lead, or association chair. The light register is correct for the lowest-effort, highest-trust ask in the set.
  - Execution — art: the robot must visibly be ours (an Edison or the actual kit, given antennae and a hover), not a generic UFO. The joke is "this is the thing your kid built, and it wants to meet your boss." A stock alien loses that second read. The line is borrowed from sci-fi cliché on purpose; the art has to carry the originality.
  - Execution — back copy: undercut the sci-fi with the small real ask, so the parent gets joke-then-clarity. Draft: "One introduction is the whole job. Your principal, your pastor, your HR team — whoever runs the room. We bring the robots; you bring the hello."

## General Promotion

- **Fun, friends and a future, close to home**
  - The core parent-facing promise. Structurally a slogan, not a title — it needs a title above it that names the actual thing (the program, the event, the class).
- **What will your kid create?**
  - Headline question. "Create" puts the kid in the author's chair; the open question invites the parent to picture something. Pairs with the tagline as question-then-answer: "What will your kid create? / Engineering Everywhere."
