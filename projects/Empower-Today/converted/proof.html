Know before you send it

Effective resolution ! The artwork is 1521×1034, which is 259 ppi at 6×4 in. The file is written at 1875×1275 / 300 dpi, so it is a 1.16× upsample — it satisfies a vendor's 300 dpi requirement but carries 259 ppi of real detail. Only a larger original would change that.
Edges The art is 1.471:1; a 6×4 card is 1.5:1, so it is 30 px narrower than the trim. The surplus is pushed entirely to the left, where the edge is flat peach, flat orange and circuit traces already meant to run off the page. The right edge — the photo — therefore gets exactly one bleed of margin, all of it trimmed away: 0 extended pixels land inside the cut on the right. Margins are the design's own edge continued outward, never mirrored (verified: zero internal variation on all four edges of both sides).
✓ Footer clearance fixed The label row now clears the cut by 37.5 px = 3.17 mm, up from 1.18 mm — exactly the standard 1/8 in safe margin. Done by stepping the left column up (logo −12, headline −30, copy −30) and growing the orange bar from 126 to 156 px, with the footer content lifted only 21 px so it re-centres in the taller bar. The orange rule stays centred between headline and copy (33 px above, 32 px below), and the rule → copy and copy → bar gaps are unchanged.
The one aesthetic cost The logo → headline gap tightened from 62 px to 41 px, so the logo reads as part of the headline stack rather than a separate mark. Widening it means lifting the logo further, which eats its top margin (already 3.51 mm). Say the word and I'll trade one for the other.
! AMAZING, sits near the right cut The comma reaches 2.33 mm from the right trim — inside the 3.175 mm safe zone by 0.85 mm. Inherited from the source art, not introduced. Fixable by nudging the tagline left if you want it.
No URL on the front Removing the QR panel took the jointheleague.org line with it. The back still carries its QR and jointheleague.org/about/impact.
Scan the printed QR The back's QR is original pixels, never redrawn — but scan the physical proof before the run.
RGB, not CMYK The PDFs are DeviceRGB. Most postcard printers convert for you, but the orange will shift unless they use a profile you approve. Ask which they want.
