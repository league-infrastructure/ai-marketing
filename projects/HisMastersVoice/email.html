His Master’s Voice

How one student taught a robot to obey the wave of a hand.

It started with an idea. Valente walked into The League’s Robot Garage and looked over the machines he’d built with his own hands — chassis he’d wired, code he’d written, motors he’d wrestled into place.

What if, he wondered, he could command them without touching a single button? At The League, that kind of question doesn’t get brushed aside. It’s exactly where every project begins — a student, a curiosity, and the freedom to chase it down.

 

Week after week, Valente built the answer: a gesture-control system that reads the motion of his hand and turns it into commands his robot understands. A wave — and the machine responds.

This is what our students actually do. Not worksheets about technology, but real engineering: sensors, code, and the hard-won confidence that comes from making something finally work.

 

Every League student has a project like this in them — they just need the tools, the space, and a mentor who believes they can. Help us put those in their hands.
