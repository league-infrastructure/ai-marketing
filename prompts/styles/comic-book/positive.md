# 1940s Comic Book — Style

Render as a 1940s Golden-Age comic-book illustration in the style of early *Superman* and
*Captain America* covers. Convert any provided photograph into hand-drawn cel-style art —
this should read as a printed vintage comic, not a modern digital illustration or a
photograph.

## Rendering
- **Linework:** Bold, confident black ink outlines with varied weight. Clean cel art —
  shapes are drawn and filled, not painted.
- **Color:** Large unbroken fields of solid FLAT color. Restricted four-color-press
  palette — roughly 5–7 ink colors total: red, blue, yellow, flesh tone, black, and cream.
  Flesh tones and warm browns (hair, skin, wood, robot metal) are allowed; this palette is
  wider and warmer than pop-art.
- **Shading:** Shadows are single flat black shapes. No hatching, no cross-hatching, no
  rendered gradients, and NO Ben-Day dot halftones (dots are the pop-art style, not this
  one).
- **Background:** Not a detailed room — one or two flat color zones only. Warm aged-
  newsprint / cream tone in cover mode, or a single saturated color field per panel in
  story mode.

## Focus & Detail
- Spend detail ONLY on (1) the child or children and (2) the invention/robot they are
  working on. Everything else simplifies to flat color and bold outline.
- Stylize faces and figures so they read as illustrated comic characters rather than
  identifiable real people — simplified inked features, not photorealistic portraits.
- Preserve the general action and the device from the source so the scene still reads as
  kids building something real.

## Text Rules
- Render ONLY the text specified for the chosen composition — exact wording, nothing else.
- Do NOT invent any other posters, signs, slogans, labels, or lettering. All other
  background surfaces are blank.

## Subject & Brand
- **Robots** are kept low-detail and iconic, like the characters — not engineering
  diagrams. Give them physically plausible front grippers, pushers, hooks, or arms. When
  robot reference photos are provided, keep the recognizable form of the real student-built
  educational robots.
- **Kids are the heroes** — proud, confident, exciting to look at.
- **Code lives only on a laptop screen facing the viewer.** No text or code anywhere else.
- **Mood:** heroic, optimistic, aspirational, exciting, competitive — kids proud of the
  robots they created.
